**Explainer (tightened)**

Retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) lets a language model answer questions with current facts without retraining the whole model. First, a search engine scans an external document store—PDFs, web pages, internal reports—and returns the most relevant passages. Then a generative model reads those passages and crafts its reply, using the retrieved text as evidence.

**Why it matters to a finance director**
- **Cost‑effective updates**: Keeping a chatbot or report‑writer current requires only re‑indexing new files, not a costly model fine‑tune. [source needed] 
- **Reduced hallucinations**: In benchmark tests, RAG cuts fabricated answers by roughly 15–30 % and improves accuracy scores, lowering the risk of costly compliance or investment errors. [source needed]

**Trade‑offs**
- Jointly training retriever and generator can improve accuracy but typically **doubles or triples** GPU compute and memory compared with fine‑tuning the generator alone. Most firms therefore **freeze the retriever** and fine‑tune only the generator to keep expenses predictable. [source needed]

**Risks & mitigations**
- **Corpus quality**: An outdated, incomplete, or biased document store will propagate those gaps. Regular corpus audits, automated freshness checks, and gating logic that discards low‑confidence passages can mitigate this, though they add operational overhead. [source needed]

**Bottom line**
RAG offers a cheaper, faster way to keep AI‑driven insights factual—provided you invest in a clean, current document store and continuously monitor retrieval quality.
